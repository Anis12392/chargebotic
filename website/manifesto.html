What we are building

Every machine runs on electricity. In the field, that electricity still comes from diesel.

Chargebotic turns any power line into a charging point. Machines take the energy they need from the grid that is already there, wherever they are working.

01 / The constraint

Range is the limit on every electric machine.

Drones, robots, sensors, radios, vehicles. The machines doing real work outside are electric, and the moment they leave a building their endurance is set by a battery. When the battery runs out, the mission stops or someone drives out to swap it.

Today the answer is a generator. Fuel to truck in, noise, heat, exhaust, and people spending their day moving energy around instead of doing the work. Solar and wind help, then the weather changes and the power stops.

Tesla's hard problem was never the car. It was the charging network.

Electric machines in the field have the same problem, and no network.

02 / The opening

The network already exists. Nobody could plug into it.

Power lines run through nearly every place where work happens. Farmland, forests, highways, industrial sites, remote terrain. They carry more energy than the machines below them could ever use.

Connecting to that energy used to mean permits, substations, trenching, and years of construction. So the grid stayed invisible to anything that moves.

We make the connection portable. A machine flies up, perches on the line, harvests energy from the magnetic field around the wire, and brings it down. Nothing is cut. Nothing touches the conductor. It is induction, the same physics as a wireless phone charger, an induction cooktop, and every transformer on the grid.

03 / How it works

Four steps, no infrastructure.

01 Perch

Land on a live line

The platform flies to an overhead conductor and clamps on. Once perched, it uses no flight power.

02 Harvest

Draw energy from the field

An inductive harvester takes power from the magnetic field around the wire. No contact, no cutting, no damage to the line.

03 Deliver

Send it where it is needed

Energy flows down a tether to a ground station, or into the aircraft's own battery.

04 Power

Keep the work running

Batteries, robots, sensors, and comms recharge in place. Silent, fuel free, no heat signature.

04 / The platform

Two machines and the software that flies them.

Portable charging system

Kestrel

Perches on the line and sends power down a tether to a ground station. It charges batteries, vehicles, and equipment where they already are.

Self charging aircraft

Spark E

Lands on the line, recharges itself, and flies on. No return to base, no crew waiting with spare batteries, far more ground covered per day.

The software finds the line, flies the approach, attaches, and harvests. It also reports what it learns: how much energy was taken, and the health of the line it was taken from.

05 / The world after this

Machines that never have to come home.

When energy is available anywhere a line runs, the rules change. A drone inspecting thousands of kilometers of infrastructure stops being a logistics exercise. A robot working a remote site keeps its sensors and comms alive for days with no fuel delivered to it. A crew in the field stops planning its day around batteries.

Fewer fuel convoys. Less noise and less heat where quiet matters. Energy that comes from the grid instead of a tank, so the work gets cleaner as the grid does.

Power is everywhere already. It has simply never been available.

We are building the layer that makes it available. An outlet wherever a line runs, and machines that know how to use it.

06 / Where we are

Proof before promise.
